# Key Features & Demo Guide

## 1. The Narrative Dashboard (Home)
The command center for the modern trader.
*   **Narrative Cards**: The left side displays the Top 5 active market narratives.
    *   *Feature*: Each card shows the Narrative Title, "Impact Score" (Sentiment), and Reliability.
    *   *Demo Point*: "Notice how we don't just show 'Gold Price Up'. We show *why*—'Central Bank Buying Spree'."
*   **Market Sidebar**: Real-time prices of Silver, Gold, and key ratios. context at a glance.

## 2. Interactive Narrative Map (The "Galaxy" View)
A visual representation of the market's mind.
*   **The dots**: Each dot represents a news article.
*   **The Colors**: Dots are colored by their cluster/narrative.
*   **Interaction**: Hover over a dot to read the specific headline.
*   **Demo Point**: "See this dense cluster here? That's the 'Green Energy Demand' narrative. See these scattered gray dots? That's just noise. We help you focus on the dense clusters."

## 3. Sentiment & Volatility Analysis
*   **Sentiment Gauge**: A visual meter showing if the narrative is Bullish (Green) or Bearish (Red).
*   **Trend Analysis**: Historical tracking of how a narrative has evolved over the last 24h.

## 4. Model Metrics Page (Transparency)
We don't just trust the "Black Box"; we verify it.
*   **Silhouette Score**: Measures how distinct the narratives are.
*   **Noise Ratio**: Shows how much data was discarded as irrelevant.
*   **Demo Point**: "We believe in explainable AI. This page proves our model is actually finding distinct patterns, not just hallucinating."

## 5. Mobile Responsiveness
*   The entire dashboard is fully responsive, allowing traders to check position drivers from their phone.

## How to Present (Demo Script Snippet)
1.  **Start at the Dashboard**: "This is the morning view. Instead of reading 50 newsletters, I see the 3 things that matter."
2.  **Click the Map**: "Let's dig deeper. This map represents 5,000 articles processed in real-time."
3.  **Highlight a Insight**: "The system found a hidden link between 'Peruvian Strikes' and 'Silver Prices' before the major news outlets did."
